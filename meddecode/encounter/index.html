No AI is involved. Every patient answer is written ahead of time so instructors can approve it. Typed and spoken questions are matched to a bank of about 45 standard interview questions (onset, what makes it better or worse, medications, allergies, last meal and so on) and 10 hands-on actions (vital signs, blood glucose, stroke check, lung sounds and others) using key words. If a question can't be matched, the patient says they don't understand and the game suggests close questions from the list. Asking the same thing in different words gets the same answer.

Speaking uses the browser's built-in speech recognition. It works in Chrome and Edge, including on Chromebooks, once the student allows the microphone. It is not available in Firefox, and it does not work inside the Claude preview. In Chrome, the audio is processed by Google's speech service, so check your district's policy before turning it on.

Scope of practice: scenarios end the way each role actually ends an encounter. EMTs choose a field impression and next action; CNAs choose what to report to the nurse and how fast; CCMAs choose the most accurate chart note for the provider; dental assistants choose what to tell the dentist. Wrong options include tasks outside the role, such as a CNA giving medication.

Scoring: key questions and actions found (50 points), the wrap-up decision (30), and three "chart it" terminology items (20). Learn mode shows medical terms as findings appear, a key-question checklist, and hints. Test mode hides them and limits the number of questions.

All scenarios are drafts for instructor review, written for education only. Follow your program's protocols and local scope of practice.
