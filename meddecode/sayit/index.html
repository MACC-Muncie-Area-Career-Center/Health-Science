How Say It works (for instructors)

Beats and stress. Each term is shown in beats from its MedDecode pronunciation (gas-TRY-tis). Students tap the beat that gets the stress before they hear it, which trains the ear for where medical words put their weight. Common patterns: -itis words stress the beat before -itis; -ology and -otomy words stress the beat before -logy or -tomy.

Listening. Hear it and Slow use the device's built-in voice. Computer voices sometimes say medical words imperfectly, so the respelling on the card is the model. Step through plays one beat at a time.

Saying it. On the MedDecode site, Record captures the student's voice in the browser so they can play it back next to the model. Nothing is uploaded or saved; the recording disappears when the next word loads. Where recording isn't allowed, students say the word aloud three times and tap each one. Check my pronunciation is optional and uses the browser's speech recognition; in Chrome, that audio goes to Google. Recognizers often miss medical words, so a miss does not mean the student was wrong.

Class echo is for the classroom screen: each word appears large, plays twice with a pause for the class to repeat, then moves on.

Team mode: finding the stress first try earns half points without ending the turn; the teacher's rating (Nailed it) earns full points and passes the turn.
