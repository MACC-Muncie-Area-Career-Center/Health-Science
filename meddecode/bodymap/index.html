How Body Map works (for instructors)

Explore shows each place's roots and every MedDecode term built from them (filtered by the Topic bar), with pronunciation. Layers keep overlapping organs apart: Outside, Organs, Urinary, Bones and Reproductive. Skin, muscle, blood, nerves and other tissues found all over the body are in the "Throughout the body" tray.

Find the root shows a root (nephr/o) and students tap where it lives. Where's the problem? shows a term (pyelonephritis) and students tap the body part it's about. Name it lights up a place and students choose its root. In the two "find" modes the map switches to the right layer for each question; a wrong tap shows what was tapped and where the answer is.

Roots that describe colors, actions or conditions (cyan/o, phag/o, lith/o) aren't places, so they aren't on the map; they appear in the other games. The figures are simplified drawings for locating organs, not an anatomy reference.

Female and male figures. Students switch with the Figure buttons (the choice is remembered on that device; ?figure=male or ?figure=female in a link sets it). The figures differ in body shape, pelvis, urethra length and reproductive organs. The Reproductive layer zooms in on the pelvis. In quiz rounds the map switches to the figure that has the answer (for example, the male figure for orchi/o) and says so. Use the Chapter filter to leave reproductive terms out of quiz rounds.

Team mode: each question is one team's turn; a correct first tap earns the points.
